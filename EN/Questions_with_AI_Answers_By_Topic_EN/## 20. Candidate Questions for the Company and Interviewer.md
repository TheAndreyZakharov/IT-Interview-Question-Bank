## 20. Questions for the Company and the Interviewer

### 20.1 Questions for the Recruiter
- Can you briefly describe what this role is and why it is open?
- Is this a new position or a replacement for someone?
- Which team and product does this role belong to?
- What is the official internal title of the position?
- What work format is expected: office, hybrid, or remote?
- What time zone does the team work in?
- In which city or country is the employee hired?
- What type of employment is offered: employment contract, contractor status, freelance agreement, outsourcing?
- Is there a probation period? How long does it last, and how is successful completion evaluated?
- How is the hiring process usually structured at the company?
- How many interview stages should I expect?
- What exactly will the stages be: screening, technical interview, live coding, system design, final interview?
- Who will participate in the next stages?
- Is there a take-home assignment? If so, how much time is usually expected for it?
- Is there live coding? In which language or stack is it best to do it?
- Is there a stage focused on algorithms, system design, or architecture?
- Which topics should I pay special attention to when preparing?
- What is the core stack for this role?
- Which technologies are mandatory, and which are nice to have?
- What level are you looking for in this position?
- What are the main criteria you will use to evaluate the candidate?
- What would be a strong signal to you that a candidate is a good fit?
- What are the most common reasons candidates get rejected for this role?
- What is the company’s timeline for closing this vacancy?
- At what stage is the hiring process currently?
- How many candidates are currently being considered?
- How quickly do you usually provide feedback after each stage?
- In what format does the company provide feedback: general or more detailed?
- Is there an opportunity to receive more detailed feedback in case of rejection?
- What are the expectations regarding English proficiency for this role?
- How often is English actually used in the job?
- Are there international teams or overseas stakeholders?
- Does the company offer relocation or relocation support?
- Is work equipment provided: laptop, monitors, reimbursements?
- Is there reimbursement for internet, coworking, learning, or fitness?
- What benefits do employees receive?
- How are vacation, sick leave, and days off handled?
- Is there a flexible schedule?
- Are working hours fixed, or do results matter more?
- How financially stable is the company?
- How long has the company been around?
- How does the company make money?
- What stage is the company at: startup, growth, mature business, corporation?
- How would you describe the company culture in a few words?
- In your opinion, what do employees like most about working here?
- What challenges or specifics about working at the company are important to know in advance?
- What style of communication and collaboration is common in the company?
- How formalized are the processes?
- How does onboarding for new employees usually work?
- Is there an onboarding program for the first weeks or months?
- Is there a mentor during onboarding?
- How often do people leave this role or this team?
- What is the reason this vacancy is open specifically?
- What are the immediate tasks for the person in this role?
- What will be most important for the new employee in the first 1–3 months?
- How would you describe the ideal candidate for this position?
- What is most valuable to you in a candidate’s resume or experience?
- How open is the company to candidates without commercial experience but with strong projects?
- How important is experience in this specific domain?
- Which teams or functions interact most often with this role?
- Are there internal levels? How does this position fit into the leveling system?
- What salary range is предусмотрed for this position?
- Is there a bonus component, performance bonus, or annual bonus?
- How often is compensation reviewed?
- Is there a transparent compensation review system?
- Are compensation reviews tied to performance reviews?
- Are there stock options, shares, or long-term incentives?
- Are there restrictions on side jobs, open source, or pet projects?
- How does the company view public activity such as blogging, conferences, or meetups?
- Is there a budget for learning and professional development?
- How supportive is the company of internal transfers between teams?
- Are there career tracks for specialists and managers?
- If you look at people who are successful in the company, what do they have in common?
- If you look at people who had a hard time in the company, what was usually the reason?
- What advice would you give a candidate to prepare better for the next stages?
- Is there anything in my profile that you would recommend I explain in more detail in the next interviews?
- Are there any questions or doubts about my background that I can clarify already now?
- If I pass all stages successfully, what will the next step be and in what timeframe?
- What personally seems to you to be the strongest argument in favor of this company?
- What would you honestly call the most difficult aspect of this role or company?
- How does the company handle market changes, layoffs, or strategy shifts?
- Have there been any significant changes in the company or team recently?
- How often do priorities change at the business level?
- How stable is the product and direction for which you are hiring?
- Are there risks of team reorganization or structural changes?
- Who makes the final hiring decision?
- At which stages does the recruiter’s opinion have the greatest impact on the final decision?
- What personally makes a candidate memorable to you at the screening stage?

### 20.2 Questions for the Hiring Manager
- Can you describe what exactly the person in this role will be responsible for?
- How would you describe the main purpose of this position?
- Why is this position open right now?
- Is this a new role or a replacement?
- How does this role impact the team and the product?
- What tasks will come in the first weeks after joining?
- Which tasks do you consider the most important in the first 1–3 months?
- What are your expectations for a new person during the probation period?
- What would be considered a successful start in this role?
- What results would you like to see from the person after 3 months?
- What results would you like to see after 6–12 months?
- Which tasks in this role are regular, and which are project-based?
- How much time usually goes to features, bug fixing, support, technical debt, and research?
- What will be the hardest part of this role in the first months?
- What are the main problems or pain points you are trying to solve by hiring for this position?
- What is currently missing in the team that you hope the new person will bring?
- How is the team structured that you are hiring into?
- How many people are on the team?
- Which roles are represented on the team?
- Who will be the closest colleagues of the new hire?
- Who will the person in this role report to directly?
- How is collaboration organized with product, design, QA, analysts, and DevOps?
- How autonomous is this role in making decisions?
- Which decisions will the person be able to make independently?
- Which decisions need to be aligned with you or others?
- How do you usually delegate tasks and responsibility?
- What management style do you personally prefer?
- How do you conduct one-on-ones and how often?
- How do you give feedback?
- How do you understand that a person is on the right track?
- What does a “strong engineer” mean to you in your team?
- What does a “strong fullstack/backend/frontend engineer” mean specifically in the context of this role?
- Which skills are critical for success in this position?
- Which gaps in knowledge are acceptable if the candidate is strong in other areas?
- What is more important to you: depth in one area or breadth across the whole stack?
- How important is product involvement in this role?
- How important is communication with stakeholders in this role?
- How much ownership is expected from the person in this role?
- What balance between delivery and quality do you consider healthy?
- How are technical decisions made in the team?
- Who defines the architectural direction?
- How are disagreements about solution design or implementation resolved?
- How do you feel about bottom-up initiatives to improve processes, architecture, or quality?
- Can you give an example of a recent decision that had a strong impact on the team or product?
- What are the team’s main technical challenges right now?
- What are the team’s main product challenges right now?
- What are the most important projects or initiatives currently underway?
- How much of the work is related to new development, and how much to supporting legacy?
- How much legacy code is there in the area this role will own?
- How would you assess the current state of the codebase?
- Which codebase problems do you consider the most painful?
- Are there any major architectural or technology migrations currently underway?
- Are there any parts of the system that are especially fragile or complex?
- What in the current system would you most like to improve first?
- Which engineering practices in the team are already working well?
- Which engineering practices would you like to strengthen?
- How is code review organized?
- What are your expectations regarding code quality in the team?
- How is testing organized?
- What level of test coverage do you consider sufficient?
- Which types of tests are actually used in day-to-day work?
- How often do incidents happen because of insufficient tests or quality control?
- How are releases organized?
- How often does the team deploy changes?
- How safe and predictable is the release process?
- How does the team respond to urgent issues and incidents?
- Who participates in incident reviews?
- How do you run postmortems and follow-up after production issues?
- How is planning organized?
- What does the work cycle look like: sprints, kanban, quarterly planning?
- How often do priorities change?
- What usually causes priority changes?
- How do you balance business tasks and technical debt?
- How do you decide when to invest in refactoring or infrastructure?
- How do you estimate timelines and risks for tasks?
- How do you handle situations when timelines start to slip?
- What counts as a strong signal for you when evaluating a candidate?
- What counts as a red flag for you?
- What mistakes do candidates most often make in interviews for this role?
- Which questions, in your experience, help distinguish a strong candidate from an average one?
- Which qualities are especially important for this team beyond technical skills?
- How important is the ability to quickly understand someone else’s code in this role?
- How important are speed versus care, structure, and thoroughness?
- Does this role include on-call or incident response duties?
- Is there an expectation that the person will lead projects, not just implement tasks?
- Is there an expectation that the person will mentor others?
- Is there an expectation that the person will participate in hiring?
- Is there an expectation that the person will influence architecture or processes?
- How do you define boundaries of responsibility among engineers in the team?
- What differentiates someone who just “does tasks” from someone who really moves the team forward?
- How do you assess engineering maturity in employees?
- What counts as a good level of independence in your team?
- What do you expect from a person when requirements are incomplete or contradictory?
- How do you expect an engineer to act if they see a technical risk no one has considered?
- What do you expect from an engineer in a conflict between “ship fast” and “do it right”?
- How much space does the team have for experimentation and improvement?
- How do you feel about proposals to change approaches, libraries, or architecture?
- What trade-offs are most typical in your team right now?
- Where is the greatest uncertainty right now?
- What about this role is most likely to surprise someone after they join?
- What in this role usually turns out to be harder than it seems during interviews?
- What, on the contrary, usually turns out to be easier or more comfortable than candidates expect?
- What kinds of people usually thrive in your team?
- What kinds of people usually do not fit in, and why?
- How would you describe the team culture?
- How open is the team to discussing mistakes and feedback?
- How common is it in the team to ask for help?
- How much written communication and documentation is there?
- How distributed is the team, and how does that affect the processes?
- How would you assess the current maturity of the team’s processes?
- If you were joining this team again today, what would you want to know in advance?
- If I joined this role and turned out to be very successful in six months, what exactly would that mean?
- If you were choosing between two strong candidates, what could become the deciding factor?
- Which topics would you recommend studying especially deeply to succeed in this role?
- What growth opportunities does this position open up in one or two years?
- Which roles do people usually grow into from this position?
- Are there strong engineers in the team or company that one can learn from?
- Which initiatives or projects give the best chance to grow quickly?
- Are there examples of people who have grown within this team?
- By which criteria do people get promoted to the next level?
- How do you determine that someone is ready for the next level?
- Which skills separate junior from middle, middle from senior, and senior from lead in your system?
- How do you view a candidate who is not equally deep across the whole stack yet but learns quickly?
- Looking specifically at my profile, what strengths do you see?
- Looking specifically at my profile, what risks or gaps would you want to understand better?
- Is there anything in my background that you would like to hear more about in order to assess fit more accurately?
- What personally makes this team and role interesting to you?
- What do you like least about this role and team, honestly speaking?
- Why do you personally like working here?
- What would you change in the team if you had additional time and resources?
- If you had to choose between speed of hiring and quality of hiring, how do you usually balance that decision?
- What will the next step be after this interview, and how will you make the decision?

### 20.3 Questions for the Team
- How is the team currently structured, and which roles does it consist of?
- How many people are on the team, and how are responsibilities divided?
- Who interacts with whom on a daily basis?
- How is collaboration organized between developers, QA, analysts, designers, and product managers?
- How cross-functional is the team?
- How long has the team existed in its current composition?
- What has changed most significantly in the team over the last six months?
- What does a typical working week look like for the team?
- What is your work rhythm: sprints, kanban, or a mixed approach?
- How many parallel tasks does one developer usually handle?
- How is task planning organized?
- How often do priorities change during work?
- How does the team react to urgent tasks and sudden context switching?
- How are daily / stand-up / sync meetings organized?
- Which meetings does the team consider useful, and which feel more forced?
- How much time does the team spend in meetings in an average week?
- Are there practices in the team that you yourselves consider overloaded or inefficient?
- How would you assess the team’s overall level of autonomy?
- To what extent do developers participate in discussing solutions rather than just receiving tasks from above?
- How are decisions made within the team?
- How much does engineers’ opinion actually influence what ultimately gets done?
- How do you usually argue about decisions: openly, through discussion, through leads?
- What happens if there is strong disagreement within the team on some issue?
- How would you describe the team’s communication style?
- How comfortable is it in the team to ask “stupid” questions?
- How do people usually ask for help?
- How quickly can you get help if you are stuck?
- How do code reviews usually work in your team?
- How many reviewers are usually involved in one pull request?
- What does the team look at first in a code review?
- What counts as a good code review in your team?
- What most often causes review comments?
- Does the team practice pair programming or joint debugging?
- How does the team feel about refactoring?
- Is there room in normal work to allocate time for improving code and infrastructure?
- What counts as “good enough” quality for a release?
- How does the team feel about tests?
- Who is mainly responsible for quality: developers, QA, or both together?
- How is testing organized before a release?
- How much does the team trust automated tests and pipelines?
- Are there releases that the team is afraid of?
- How often does the team release changes?
- How routine are releases, or is it stressful every time?
- How do incidents typically happen on your team: are they rare or a regular part of life?
- How does the team behave during a production incident?
- Do you have a blameless postmortem culture?
- What usually causes the most pain for the team: the codebase, processes, dependent teams, deadlines?
- What is the team’s main source of frustration right now?
- On the other hand, what works especially well in the team?
- Why do people like working here specifically?
- What keeps strong engineers on your team?
- Why do you think people leave the team?
- Who would be successful on this team, and who would struggle?
- What qualities are especially valued in a new person joining your team?
- What most often distinguishes people who integrate into the team quickly?
- What mistakes should a newcomer avoid in their first few months?
- What does onboarding a new developer look like?
- On average, how long does it take before someone delivers their first useful task to production?
- When do you know that a person has successfully integrated into the team?
- Is there documentation that genuinely helps a new person?
- What usually turns out to be the most unexpected thing for newcomers?
- How dependent are you on individual strong people?
- Do you have bus factor problems?
- How evenly is knowledge about the system distributed?
- How does the team share knowledge?
- Do you do internal tech docs, demos, or knowledge-sharing sessions?
- How do you usually document decisions?
- How do you know that the team is moving in the right direction?
- What metrics or signals do you look at within the team?
- How does the team measure its effectiveness, if at all?
- How focused is the team on business outcomes rather than just closing tasks?
- Does the team feel a connection between its work and user outcomes?
- Does the team have influence over the roadmap?
- What is considered success on the team?
- What is considered failure on the team?
- How is collaboration with neighboring teams organized?
- Where do problems most often arise at the boundaries between teams?
- How easy is it to align with neighboring teams on priorities and dependencies?
- Are there regular conflicts between engineering and product / QA / support / analytics?
- How are such conflicts usually resolved?
- How is on-call support or business-request support organized?
- Do developers participate in support after release?
- How often are developers interrupted by ad-hoc requests?
- What is the balance between new features and supporting the existing system?
- How much time goes into legacy-related tasks?
- How understandable and predictable is the codebase to work with?
- Are there parts of the system that everyone is afraid to touch?
- How does the team live with technical debt?
- Does the team have time to improve internal tools?
- How do you feel about experimenting with new approaches and tools?
- How conservative is the team in technical decisions?
- How much bottom-up initiative is encouraged?
- If I come in with an idea to improve a process or system, how likely is it that it would actually be considered?
- Does the team feel ownership over the product and the system?
- What would the team like to improve in the next 3–6 months?
- If you could change one thing about how the team works right now, what would it be?
- How would you describe the feedback culture on the team?
- How openly do people give each other feedback?
- Are there toxic patterns the team is actively dealing with?
- How safe is it to admit a mistake on the team?
- How does the team treat people with different levels of experience?
- Is there a sense of mentorship and support for juniors / mid-level engineers?
- How does the team help people grow technically?
- How does the team help people grow in product and communication skills?
- What topics come up most often in retrospectives?
- Do retrospectives lead to real changes, or do they mostly stay at the level of discussion?
- What does a typically successful developer on your team look like after six months?
- What advice would you give to someone joining your team?
- What do you wish you had known about the team before joining?

### 20.4 Questions for the Tech Lead / Architect
- How would you describe the current system architecture as a whole?
- What are the main components or services in the system?
- Where are the key boundaries between modules or services?
- What architectural style is currently used, and why was it chosen?
- If you were designing the system from scratch today, what would you do differently?
- Which architectural decisions have been the most successful?
- Which architectural decisions currently cause the most pain?
- What are the most difficult technical trade-offs in the system?
- What is considered the most fragile part of the architecture right now?
- Are there parts of the system the team is afraid to change?
- What are the main constraints of the system that you take into account during development?
- What currently limits product development speed the most: architecture, processes, infrastructure, or people?
- How are responsibilities divided between teams at the architectural level?
- How clearly are ownership and service / module boundaries defined?
- Where do architectural conflicts between teams arise most often?
- How do you make architectural decisions?
- Who makes the final decision on technical matters?
- How centralized or distributed are architectural decisions across teams?
- Do you use ADRs, RFCs, or other decision-recording practices?
- How do you document architecture?
- Is architecture documentation alive and maintained, or more formal and static?
- How often is the architecture reviewed?
- Do you have regular architecture reviews?
- How do you know that an architectural decision turned out to be wrong?
- What signals tell you it is time to change the architecture?
- How do you approach evolving the system without doing a big rewrite from scratch?
- How common is it to make incremental changes instead of large rewrites?
- What major migrations or architectural changes have happened recently?
- What major migrations are planned in the near future?
- What architectural risks do you consider the most serious over the next year?
- How do you balance delivery speed and architectural sustainability?
- How do you decide when something is “good enough” versus when you need to invest in a stronger solution?
- How does the company decide to invest in technical debt reduction and platform improvements?
- How easy is it to justify architectural investments to the business?
- Do you have a technical strategy for the next 6–12 months?
- If so, what are its main directions?
- How is the technical strategy connected to business goals?
- How ready is the architecture for product growth right now?
- Where are the system’s current scaling bottlenecks?
- Which performance constraints matter most to you?
- Where do you usually hit limits: CPU, memory, network, database, or external integrations?
- What approaches do you use to scale the system?
- Which parts of the system scale most easily, and which scale the worst?
- Are there stateful components in the system that make scaling harder?
- How do you approach high availability and fault tolerance?
- Which failure scenarios do you consider critical?
- Which real incidents had the biggest impact on architectural decisions?
- What lessons has the architecture learned from incidents?
- How do you approach disaster recovery and fallback scenarios?
- What is your situation with RPO/RTO, and how realistic are those targets?
- How do you design the system to handle failures of external services?
- What approaches do you use for graceful degradation?
- Do you use circuit breakers, retries, backoff, and bulkheads, and where are they especially important?
- How do you protect against cascading failures?
- How do you decide between synchronous and asynchronous communication?
- Where do you use REST, where messaging, where event-driven approaches, and why?
- Do you use queues or streaming, and for what scenarios?
- How do you handle idempotency and message reprocessing?
- How do you design transaction boundaries?
- How do you deal with distributed consistency?
- Where do you need strong consistency, and where is eventual consistency acceptable?
- Do you use outbox / saga / compensating actions?
- What trade-offs do you usually explain to the team or the business in these decisions?
- How do you design and version APIs?
- Which API design principles are considered mandatory for you?
- How strictly are contract rules between services and teams followed?
- Do you use schema evolution / backward compatibility / contract testing?
- How do you prevent breaking changes?
- How do you approach API versioning?
- Where in the architecture do you use GraphQL, gRPC, or only REST?
- How do you choose data exchange formats: JSON, Protobuf, Avro, etc.?
- What rules do you have for errors, timeouts, retries, and SLAs between services?
- How do you design caching?
- Where does caching provide the most value in your system?
- What caching problems have been the most painful?
- How do you handle invalidation, stampede, and stale data?
- How do you approach data storage at the architectural level?
- Why were these particular databases and storage systems chosen?
- Where do you use PostgreSQL / MySQL / MongoDB / Redis / ClickHouse / Elasticsearch, and why?
- How do you decide when a new type of storage is needed versus when the existing one is enough?
- Do you have polyglot persistence in the system? How much does it help or hurt?
- What are the hardest data problems right now: consistency, performance, schema, or data volume growth?
- How do you approach schema migrations without downtime?
- What safe database change rules do you have?
- How well is the architecture adapted for teams to release independently?
- Are there common components or shared libraries that slow down independence?
- Where are your strongest coupling points?
- What architectural smells do you see in the system?
- How do you evaluate module boundaries and bounded contexts?
- Do you use DDD in real work or only some of its ideas?
- Where does a domain model help, and where does it only make things more complicated?
- How much is the architecture oriented around the product domain rather than team structure?
- How do you approach modularity in a monolith?
- If you have microservices, which microservice architecture problems have been the most painful?
- If you have a monolith, what are its strengths and what limitations have become noticeable?
- How do you decide whether to “keep the monolith” or “decompose” it?
- How do you deal with distributed complexity?
- What level of engineering maturity does the team need for the chosen architecture to actually work?
- How do you build observability into the architecture?
- Which metrics and logs do you consider mandatory by default?
- How well can requests currently be traced across the system?
- What observability problems make incident investigation hardest?
- How do you build operability into system design instead of adding it after the fact?
- How do you design the system so that it is easy to support and debug?
- How do you feel about platform engineering / internal platforms / developer experience?
- Do you have internal platform teams or internal tools?
- Which internal tools help developers the most?
- Which internal tools would you most like to improve first?
- How do you evaluate developer experience in the current system?
- How easy is it for a new engineer to understand the system and start working in it?
- How do you approach security by design?
- Who is responsible for the security of architectural decisions?
- What security requirements do you incorporate at the design stage?
- Do you do threat modeling for critical changes?
- Which classes of vulnerabilities are most relevant to you?
- How do you design access control between services and components?
- How are secrets management, key rotation, and auditability handled?
- How do you account for privacy and PII at the architectural level?
- What restrictions exist on logging and data retention?
- How do you balance security and developer convenience?
- How much do infrastructure decisions affect architectural constraints?
- How do you interact with DevOps / SRE / Platform teams?
- Who is responsible for Nginx / ingress / service mesh / Kubernetes decisions when they affect architecture?
- How dependent is the application on a cloud provider or specific stack?
- Is there a risk of vendor lock-in, and how much does it concern you?
- How do you feel about serverless / managed services / self-hosted solutions in your context?
- How do you make build vs buy decisions?
- Where did you consciously choose an off-the-shelf solution instead of building your own?
- Where did you have to build your own solution instead, and why?
- Which architectural decisions were driven not by engineering but by business constraints, team limitations, or deadlines?
- How well is the architecture adapted to the current team size?
- If the team doubled in size, what would become the bottleneck?
- If traffic grew 10x, where would the first serious risk be?
- If tomorrow you had to launch in another country / region / segment, what would need to change?
- How does the architecture help or hinder the fast launch of new features?
- How do you evaluate time-to-market under the current architecture?
- Are there architectural decisions the team regrets but still cannot fix?
- Which decisions did you consciously postpone “for later,” and why?
- Do you have a list of architectural debts or a risk register?
- What do you consider a good engineer or senior engineer in the context of your architecture?
- What level of independence do you expect from a new person in technical decisions?
- In which areas will a new person be able to influence the architecture?
- How much initiative do you expect from developers in improving the system?
- What would be a strong signal to you that a candidate will fit well into your engineering environment?
- What typical mistakes in architectural thinking do you most often see in candidates?
- What would you like to hear from a candidate when they reason about system design?
- What questions about your system do you consider the best ones a candidate can ask?
- What do you personally like most about the current architecture?
- What would you most like to change in it first?

### 20.5 Questions about Product and Business
- How does the company make money from this product?
- What are the product’s main revenue streams?
- Is the product profitable, or is it still in a phase of active investment for growth?
- What are the main business goals for the product over the next 6–12 months?
- How do you define the product’s core value for the user?
- Who is the product’s main user or customer?
- Which user segments are the most important for you?
- What is the product’s ICP / ideal customer profile?
- How do you describe the key user personas?
- What is the main user problem the product solves?
- Which product use cases are most critical for the business?
- Which features or directions are considered strategic for the product?
- What is currently the main driver of product growth?
- Which product metrics are considered key?
- Which North Star Metric or product KPIs do you use?
- Which business metrics matter most to the engineering team?
- How does the engineering team influence product and business metrics?
- Which product decisions recently had the biggest impact?
- Which hypotheses or experiments are currently in progress?
- How do you determine that a new feature is truly successful?
- How do you measure the success of a release or new functionality?
- Which metrics do you look at after launching new features?
- How are decisions made about what to build next in the product?
- Who makes the final decision on priorities: product, business, engineering, or someone else?
- How does the company balance short-term business needs with long-term product development?
- How often is the roadmap revisited?
- How stable is the roadmap, and how often do priorities shift sharply?
- How do you decide whether to invest in a new feature versus improving the current product?
- How do you decide between growing new capabilities and fixing existing product problems?
- How strongly is the roadmap influenced by requests from specific customers?
- Do you have enterprise customers that significantly affect the roadmap?
- How much is the product driven by users, data, sales requests, or founder vision?
- How do you work with user feedback?
- Which channels of user feedback are most important to you?
- How much access does the engineering team have to users and customer insights?
- Do developers have the opportunity to participate in discovery or talk to users?
- How often do engineers get involved in product discovery?
- How do you validate product hypotheses before development?
- Do you use A/B testing, feature flags, canary rollout, user research?
- What major product risks do you see right now?
- Which business risks are most critical for the product over the next year?
- What competitive advantages does the product have in the market?
- Who are your main competitors?
- Where does your product outperform competitors today?
